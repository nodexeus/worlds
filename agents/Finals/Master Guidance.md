NODEXEUS
AI Agent Character Universe
Master Character Design Brief — Version 2.0
Purpose: Character concept development and reusable 3D artwork
1. Project Overview
Nodexeus is developing an agentic AI platform featuring a virtual 3D world populated by distinctive AI characters.
Each character represents an AI agent performing a particular function within the platform.
These characters move around the virtual environment, interact with objects, work, rest, communicate through facial expressions, and display personality.
We are creating a growing collection of unique characters that share a recognizable creative universe without necessarily sharing the same anatomy, proportions, construction, or appearance.
Our objective is to create an imaginative cast of memorable characters—not a collection of generic robot mascots.
The characters will eventually be recreated as 3D models, but this initial project focuses exclusively on their visual design and identity.
Primary creative principle
Creativity and originality take priority over technical implementation.
Do not restrict character concepts to simple geometry, common humanoid skeletons, or existing animation systems.
We will address modeling and animation requirements after a character's visual design has been approved.
2. Visual Style and Creative Direction
Our target aesthetic is:
Premium cinematic CGI featuring imaginative mechanical characters with playful personalities, unconventional anatomy, and physically convincing materials.
The characters should feel like inhabitants of a sophisticated, slightly whimsical science-fiction universe.
They should be interesting enough to stand alone as works of character art.
Desired characteristics
Imaginative
- Unexpected silhouettes and unusual constructions.
- Creative combinations of mechanical, technological, and unconventional elements.
- Designs that feel original rather than derivative of common robot archetypes.
Playful
- Goofy personalities are encouraged.
- Oversized eyes, unusual heads, exaggerated proportions, and awkward anatomy are welcome.
- Characters can be charming, curious, mischievous, intelligent, clumsy, sophisticated, or eccentric.
- Personality should be apparent even in a static pose.
Sophisticated
- Premium CGI rendering quality.
- Physically convincing materials and construction.
- Beautiful lighting, reflections, and surface treatments.
- Attention to mechanical believability and interesting visual details.
Distinctive
- Every character should have its own immediately recognizable silhouette.
- Different characters should have noticeably different proportions, facial designs, and physical structures.
- Distinctiveness should come from anatomy and construction, not just color or accessories.
Expressive
- Characters need personality.
- Facial expressions can involve optical lenses, mechanical eyelids, articulated components, illuminated displays, or other imaginative mechanisms.
- Traditional human facial anatomy is not required.
3. Critical Distinction: Fun vs. Cartoonish
This is one of our most important creative requirements.
We want cartoon-like personality without generic cartoon character design.
Encouraged
- Oversized expressive eyes.
- Exaggerated physical proportions.
- Quirky and unusual mechanical anatomy.
- Asymmetrical features.
- Interesting combinations of shapes and materials.
- Awkward, endearing, or slightly ridiculous appearances.
- Imaginative facial mechanisms.
- Unconventional methods of movement.
Avoid
- Generic Pixar-style robot mascots.
- Plastic toy aesthetics.
- Identical rounded heads with smiling LED screens.
- Repetitive humanoid robots with different armor colors.
- Characters whose only distinguishing feature is an accessory.
- Excessively smooth, simplified, childlike designs.
- Designs that resemble common consumer robots with minor modifications.
A character can have enormous eyes and an oversized head without looking like a children's cartoon.
The difference comes from the sophistication of its construction, materials, proportions, and overall artistic execution.
4. Character Anatomy and Design Freedom
There are very few restrictions on the physical form of these characters.
Consider possibilities such as:
- Mechanical insectoids.
- Unusual robotic creatures.
- Floating mechanical entities.
- Characters constructed around glass domes or transparent enclosures.
- Asymmetric mechanical beings.
- Unconventional humanoids.
- Industrial or retro-futuristic mechanical constructs.
- Characters with multiple optical systems.
- Characters incorporating unusual materials or internal mechanisms.
- Designs with unexpected relationships between their heads, bodies, and limbs.
Mechanical creatures are encouraged
A mechanical insect with oversized optical eyes, articulated appendages, and weathered metal plating fits our aesthetic.
An organic owl wearing a business suit does not.
Do not generate organic mammals, birds, or other conventional animals dressed as people.
Creature-inspired designs should be clearly mechanical, artificial, or otherwise distinctly nonorganic.
Locomotion
Do not assume every character needs conventional legs.
Potential approaches include:
- Traditional articulated legs.
- Multiple mechanical legs.
- Wheels.
- Hovering or levitation.
- Mechanical appendages that support several types of movement.
- Unconventional combinations of these systems.
The character's locomotion should feel intentional and consistent with its physical construction.
Do not attach random mechanical legs or wheels simply to satisfy a presumed animation requirement.
5. Character Diversity
The collection will eventually include 15 or more characters.
Some shared visual features are acceptable and even desirable. They help establish a cohesive universe.
However, individual characters must not feel like reskins of one another.
Diversity should come from
Element	Opportunities
Silhouette	Tall, short, wide, narrow, asymmetric, unusual
Head	Optical arrays, domes, articulated structures, geometric forms
Eyes	Single, paired, multiple, different shapes and mechanisms
Body	Segmented, suspended, monolithic, exposed machinery
Limbs	Heavy, delicate, telescoping, unconventional
Movement	Walking, rolling, hovering, unusual mechanical motion
Materials	Metal, glass, ceramic, stone, translucent surfaces
Personality	Goofy, serious, curious, confident, eccentric
Color	Distinct palettes appropriate to each design


The silhouette test
Imagine removing all colors, materials, accessories, and facial details.
Would the character still be recognizable?
Ideally, yes.
Character identity should come primarily from physical form, not accessories.
However, shared components are not forbidden. With a large cast, some overlap is natural.
6. Materials and Color
The Nodexeus visual universe should feature premium, physically believable materials.
Recommended material directions
- Weathered painted metal.
- Brushed aluminum.
- Aged copper and brass.
- Black anodized metal.
- Industrial ceramics.
- Reflective or translucent glass.
- Stone and mineral surfaces.
- Illuminated optical components.
- Subtle fabric elements.
- Unexpected combinations of conventional and futuristic materials.
Surface characteristics
Materials may feature:
- Realistic wear and scratches.
- Subtle dirt and patina.
- Interesting reflections.
- Mechanical seams and fasteners.
- Illumination from internal components.
- Layered surface treatments.
Avoid making every character look factory-new or manufactured from shiny plastic.
Color freedom
Every character can have a unique color palette.
Nodexeus traditionally uses black, charcoal, and gold/amber, but these are not mandatory primary colors for every character.
Explore:
- Turquoise and aged copper.
- Pink and graphite.
- Deep purple and brass.
- Burgundy and dark steel.
- Petrol blue and bronze.
- Olive and industrial gray.
- Cream ceramic and dark metal.
- Unexpected accent colors.
Colors should complement the character's construction and personality.
Do not force all characters into the same color scheme.

Important reference instructions

They are not templates.
Do not recreate an existing character with a different head, body color, or accessory.
When reference images are provided, understand which aspects demonstrate artistic style and which are unique to the original character.
A new character should fit the same creative universe without copying existing designs.
8. Future 3D World Integration
These characters will eventually inhabit an interactive virtual world.
The current world supports:
- Walking and moving around.
- Standing and idle behavior.
- Sitting.
- Sleeping and resting.
- Working with a hammer.
- Expressing emotions.
- Interacting with their surroundings.
Existing characters share a common animation rig.
However, this is not a restriction on new character concepts.
We can adapt or develop additional animation systems to accommodate compelling designs.
Design philosophy
Do not reject an interesting character simply because its anatomy is unusual.
Do not simplify a concept prematurely to accommodate Blender.
Do not force conventional humanoid limbs onto characters that would be more interesting without them.
We will determine how to implement the design after it has been approved.
The initial character should be the best visual design we can imagine, not the easiest model we can build.
9. Current Deliverables
This phase is exclusively about creating the character library.
We are not currently producing marketing campaigns.
Primary deliverable: Standalone Character
Generate one high-resolution image featuring a single character.
Requirements:
- Full-body composition.
- One character only.
- Genuine transparent background.
- No scenery or environmental elements.
- No text, typography, names, or labels.
- No character names printed on clothing or equipment.
- No marketing slogans.
- No invented logos.
- Premium cinematic CGI quality.
- Excellent lighting and material detail.
- Clear presentation of the character's physical design.
Resolution: Prefer 2048 pixels or higher when supported.
Important transparency requirement
The background must have a genuine alpha channel.
Do not generate a checkerboard pattern to simulate transparency.
If transparency cannot be generated directly, remove the background from the approved image while preserving the original character.
Never redesign the character merely to remove the background.
Secondary deliverable: Additional Character Pose
Once the character design is approved, generate an optional second pose.
Examples:
- Neutral standing or idle pose.
- Expressive or playful pose.
- Curious or thinking pose.
- Dynamic action pose.
The second image must preserve the exact character identity.
Do not change its physical construction, materials, colors, or distinctive features between poses.
Additional poses should be generated only when requested.
10. Marketing Artwork — Future Phase
Marketing artwork is deliberately excluded from the current generation phase.
However, the character designs should be suitable for future use in sophisticated promotional campaigns.
Future marketing artwork may include:
- Cinematic environments.
- Role-specific workspaces.
- Promotional slogans.
- Nodexeus branding.
- Typography and marketing messaging.
- Props, equipment, and environmental storytelling.
- Sophisticated lighting and compositions.
Slogans and creative marketing copy are encouraged in future marketing illustrations.
They are an important part of the visual identity we've established.
However, character-specific names should not be introduced into the illustrations unless explicitly requested.
The initial transparent character artwork should remain completely free of typography.
11. Image Generation Workflow
This workflow is mandatory to minimize unnecessary generations, cost, and wasted time.
Step 1 — Establish the concept
Identify the character's intended role and broad personality.
Develop a distinctive visual direction.
Avoid excessive planning or lengthy preliminary discussion.
If the user has already provided sufficient direction, proceed directly to generation.
Step 2 — Generate one character
Produce one standalone character image.
Do not automatically generate multiple variations.
Do not create character grids, contact sheets, or combined marketing artwork.
Step 3 — User review
Wait for feedback.
If approved, treat the design as authoritative.
If changes are requested, make the specific changes rather than redesigning the entire character.
Step 4 — Optional additional pose
Only after approval, and only when requested.
Use the approved character image as the visual reference.
Preserve its physical identity.
Step 5 — Move to the next character
Once approved assets are complete, begin a fresh session for the next character.
Do not unnecessarily carry over failed designs or unrelated artwork from previous sessions.
12. Cost and Efficiency Requirements
Minimizing wasted generations is a critical requirement.
Follow these rules:
1. One character at a time. Never generate a collection unless explicitly requested.
2. One generation per request. Do not automatically retry.
3. No unsolicited variations. Wait for approval.
4. No unnecessary narration. Generate the image rather than repeatedly explaining what will be generated.
5. No repeated failures. If the generator ignores a critical instruction, stop rather than repeatedly submitting similar prompts.
6. Protect approved designs. Use references rather than recreating characters from descriptions.
7. Do not confuse props with identity. A character's profession does not need to dictate its anatomy.
8. Do not generate marketing artwork during the character-design phase.
9. Do not show multiple near-identical designs and ask the user to choose between them unless variations were requested.
10. Do not claim an asset is finished unless the requested output actually exists and meets the requirements.
Communication expectations
Be concise.
Avoid repeated explanations of design principles that have already been established.
If the user asks for an image, create the image.
If a generation is unsuccessful, explain the issue briefly and wait for instructions.
The objective is creative output, not lengthy discussions about creative output.
13. Approval and Character Consistency
Once a character is approved, its appearance becomes authoritative.
All subsequent artwork must preserve:
- Fundamental anatomy.
- Head and facial construction.
- Body proportions.
- Mechanical features.
- Materials.
- Colors.
- Distinctive characteristics.
A different pose is not a different character.
When an approved reference image exists, use it directly.
Do not rely solely on text descriptions to recreate an existing character.
Keep approved designs separate from unsuccessful concepts.
14. Starting a New Character Session
The following instructions can be pasted into a new conversation alongside this master brief and any relevant visual references.
New Session Prompt
We are designing a new character for the Nodexeus AI Agent Universe.
Follow the attached Master Character Design Brief.
Create one original, imaginative mechanical character with a distinctive silhouette, expressive personality, unconventional physical design, and premium cinematic CGI rendering quality.
The character should feel playful, interesting, and perhaps slightly goofy, but must not resemble a generic cartoon robot mascot.
Use the supplied reference images to understand the creative quality and visual universe we're establishing, not to copy existing character anatomy.
Prioritize originality and imagination over modeling simplicity or animation compatibility.
For this phase, generate only a single full-body character on a genuinely transparent background.
Do not include text, names, slogans, labels, marketing graphics, or environmental backgrounds.
Do not generate multiple variations or additional poses unless requested.
Do not generate marketing artwork.
Minimize commentary and unnecessary tool usage.
Generate the character now.

15. Guiding Creative Principle
This is the most important instruction in the entire document:
We are creating fascinating characters that happen to represent AI agents—not designing AI agents that happen to look like robots.
Their professional functions shouldn't determine their anatomy.
Their physical designs should be imaginative, surprising, expressive, and memorable.
Some characters might be sleek and sophisticated. Others might be awkward, goofy, eccentric, or mechanically bizarre.
They should all feel like they belong in the same imaginative universe.
The goal is to make someone look at each character and think, "That's awesome. What is that thing?"
Not, "There's another robot with a different color."
